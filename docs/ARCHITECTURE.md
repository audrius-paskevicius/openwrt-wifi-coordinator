# Current architecture

The daemon in `channel-coordinator/files/main.uc` runs an observe → plan → execute → verify cycle. Hardware calls go through an injected interface in the survey and executor modules, allowing tests without moving live radios. Radio Preference is a separate service in the same APK; a shared lease prevents its preferred-radio steering from interfering with scans, preparation and channel moves.

## Surveying

`survey.uc` collects full broad sweeps. Paced mode visits one frequency at a time and does not require traffic silence. It prefers empty radios; among occupied radios it estimates interrupted client capacity from negotiated rates, traffic and measured scan duration. It waits for the least-cost scanner rather than borrowing a costlier radio during its serving gap. A ready frequency on another band can be scanned meanwhile.

Defaults are a 10% scan-duty budget and at least three seconds of serving time between occupied-radio visits. Active probes are used only where allowed; DFS and other restricted frequencies use passive listening. Empty-only and sustained-quiet modes remain supported. The latter uses the same traffic threshold and quiet duration as quiet-only channel changes.

Only radios with complete, fresh coverage of their candidate spectrum may move. An incomplete other band does not block a fully measured band. Missing frequencies are unknown, not clear spectrum. On 2.4 GHz, scans also cover neighboring frequencies whose networks can overlap allowed candidate channels.

## Planning and evidence

`policy.uc` supplies geometry, regulatory checks and the neighbor-pressure calculation shared with history graphs. `planner.uc` is the single planning implementation. It evaluates driver-advertised HT/VHT widths (20/40 MHz on 2.4 GHz and 20/40/80/160 MHz on 5 GHz), with at most four radios and a bounded search of 100,000 nodes. If the bound is exceeded, the decision holds the current layout rather than executing a partially searched result. HT40 considers both secondary-channel directions. VHT160 uses standard contiguous blocks, not sliding centers or 80+80. AUTO_BW can join contiguous regulatory ranges while preserving per-member width, power and DFS restrictions.

The `channels` setting defaults to `auto`. Each inventory refresh resolves it from the radio's current nl80211 frequency capabilities, confined to its active band. Disabled frequencies and non-DFS frequencies prohibiting AP initiation are excluded. DFS frequencies remain subject to DFS mode, kernel state and clearance checks. An explicit numeric list restricts primary channels, independently of width: listing 36 can permit an 80 MHz block with primary 36 without also listing 40, 44 and 48. Every member frequency must still pass driver, regulatory and DFS checks. No fixed 1–11 or 5 GHz channel list limits automatic discovery. The controller still supports the HT/VHT bands described above, not 6 GHz.

For each 20 MHz member of a candidate block, estimated congestion is the larger of scaled neighbor pressure and available external busy-airtime evidence, capped at 100. The candidate's occupancy is their mean. Busy counters on channels carrying our clients are excluded from planning because they include our own traffic. Missing counters do not add invented congestion; the neighbor estimate remains available.

The service estimate follows nested primary 20/40/80/160 MHz blocks. For each block, neighbour pressure sums each BSS's strongest overlap with any member once, so one wide BSS is not counted repeatedly. The largest available external busy counter can increase this estimate. These are interference proxies, not independent busy probabilities. Incremental bandwidth is weighted by the resulting free fraction: `20*f20 + 20*f40 + 40*f80 + 80*f160`, stopping at the candidate width. This models narrower transmissions when secondary spectrum is busy and makes a clean primary useful to narrow clients too. Mean occupancy remains a separate display value, not the ranking objective.

Radar risk discounts that usable bandwidth by `1 - 0.75*risk`. Service index is `100 * usable_MHz * advertised_bidirectional_streams / reference_MHz`, where the fixed reference is 40 MHz for 2.4 GHz and 160 MHz for 5 GHz. HT and VHT MCS capabilities supply stream counts; absent or undecoded capabilities conservatively use one. The reference is shared within a band, so a capable main radio and a single-stream spare are not normalized into equals. Index values can exceed 100. Internal cost is `100 - service` and can be negative.

This is a stable hardware-headroom objective, independent of current client counts, current offered traffic and transient negotiated rates. It preserves room for future clients; it does not assume that each client can use all streams or the full configured width. Existing clients still constrain execution and connectivity recovery. No client capability database, new dependency or additional flash writes are introduced. The model cannot predict individual signal quality, hidden nodes, actual throughput or future demand. Its bonded-channel structure follows the primary/secondary behavior described in [Cisco's RRM white paper](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/wireless/controller/technotes/8-3/b_RRM_White_Paper/dca.html); the service score is this project's heuristic, not Cisco's algorithm.

Joint layouts rank by least self-overlap when separation is enabled, then greatest total service. Width and legal transmit power break equal-score ties. If separation is disabled, overlap instead competes through the configured cost penalty. There is no width-first ranking, congestion cutoff for narrowing or radar-risk cliff. Primary frequency is part of placement identity. The planner evaluates all eligible primaries, retaining the best primary per footprint for joint search (incumbent on ties). Width tie-breaking sums each width divided by that radio's maximum configured/current width; it has no authority to override a service difference. Recovery exclusions still cover the whole failed footprint, so changing primary cannot evade them.

Transitions may use one neutral staging move; with separation enabled, no intermediate move may increase overlap. The next step is reevaluated after each completed operation. Among executable moves, the radio with the oldest last attempt/completed move goes first, independent of its name or enumeration order. This prevents an unrelated radio with repeated width fallbacks from starving a multi-step placement.

Evidence uncertainty starts at one percentage point with complete measured airtime, otherwise three. Changes in usable capacity between surveys can raise it to 15; a changed scanner imposes a floor of six. It is converted to service-index units using the radio's maximum width and advertised streams. Each alternative retains its own consecutive supporting-survey count, even if another alternative ranks first. For each changed radio, comparison uncertainty is the larger of current and target uncertainty, divided by the square root of its supporting comparisons. Required benefit is the greater of the configured minimum (default three index points) and the largest of those per-radio margins. Missing, stale or contrary evidence resets support. Only improved separation can bypass the service margin. Equal-service ties do not trigger moves. Admitted multi-step plans do not repeatedly pay the admission margin while their remaining benefit stays positive. Radar memory decays continuously, including fractional half-lives.

The last completed comparison is retained in the RAM state file across surveys, moves and service restarts within the same boot. It is explanatory evidence, never an execution queue. Sampling marks placement changes; the UI shows evaluation time, age and current progress, and labels all placement, pool and exclusion data as belonging to that snapshot. A completed evaluation replaces it.

The comparison table shows individual alternatives sorted by sharing with peer placements at comparison time, then decreasing service index. Estimated usable width is before stream weighting but after the radar discount; primary/block congestion shows per-primary and mean block estimates. These are not percentages of measured airtime. The table is not the joint-plan ranking: the joint plan can also move peers.

## Execution, clients and timing

`executor.uc` changes one radio at a time. It rechecks configuration, regulatory eligibility and peer overlap immediately before execution. It uses CSA when supported. A restart first attempts a same-band, same-network handoff; if none exists, it skips that wait. With the shared reconnect fallback enabled, it can restart the selected single-AP radio when clients remain. Protected clients and explicit quiet-only/empty-only policies still restrict restart operations.

Handoff defaults to 90 seconds, progressing from gentle requests to stronger requests and then a bounded disconnect if decisive escalation is enabled. Temporary return bans are cleared before the reconnect fallback. Recovery uses reachable private-LAN ping targets, bidirectional data counters and associations. Missing clients get a 60-second reconnection grace period before persistent-failure handling; unprobeable clients require a longer association observation. Association alone does not establish working internet. Confirmed radio failures trigger rollback and six-hour destination exclusions. Client checks use the scoped recovery policy below. Interrupted operations restore their recovery journal, then observe enabled, previously known APs for 30 seconds of stable channel state before automatically resuming. Disabled APs do not block this check. No client traffic or roaming is required, and the observation itself does not restart radios. Recovery is blocked while a journal remains, an AP is missing or inconsistent, or a previously recorded per-radio failure remains unresolved. After 780 seconds (the fixed internal `cac_timeout` of 720 plus 60), an unsuccessful check becomes an attention-required stop. A journal restoration error can stop recovery immediately. A previously failed radio is not automatically cleared merely because it is now associated with clients. Another service restart cannot clear a genuine recovery error. Successful verification clears stale radio-unavailable flags but preserves failed-destination and client restrictions. This checks AP readiness, not end-to-end internet access.

When HT40 setup retains 20 MHz, the observed width constraint applies across that radio's primary channels for one evidence-freshness window (`max_age`, normally 15 minutes). Moving to another primary does not immediately retry the same unavailable width. The UI reports the reason and remaining time; configured widths and hardware capabilities remain unchanged. The comparison retains the constraint applicable when evaluated.

Client recovery distinguishes a failed radio, a working AP with an affected client, and inconclusive evidence. Fresh per-AP bidirectional data or a successful probe with an unambiguous association establishes positive service evidence. An association alone cannot verify an alternate AP. When other clients demonstrate that the destination works, only affected clients receive bounded handoff requests to a compatible same-band AP with matching security and LAN. Existing escalation, signal checks, protected-client rules and the shared five-minute forced-disconnect budget apply. If alternate service verifies, the new placement remains available to healthy clients.

A successful selective recovery records a six-hour client/radio/placement restriction in a private, same-boot RAM file, capped at 256 entries. Radio Preference and channel handoffs avoid recommending that combination; voluntary association is still allowed. The coordinator tries a verified preflight handoff before reusing it for that client. Without a permitted, verified fallback, the placement is deferred for six hours. Records expire automatically and are never archived to flash; history contains only anonymous counts and outcomes.

A failed client check does not by itself disable CSA. A working AP without a verified client fallback receives a six-hour compatibility deferral. Inconclusive checks initially defer the placement for one evidence-freshness interval (`max_age`, normally 15 minutes); positive recovery of the affected client on the previous placement promotes this to six hours. Recovery never bounces from the restored original placement back to the failed destination. If the restored AP demonstrably serves other clients and only unprotected, absent clients remain unconfirmed, planning pauses for one freshness interval rather than marking the radio permanently failed. Otherwise unresolved recovery still requires attention. No check here establishes end-to-end internet performance.

Default timing is 60 seconds between completed surveys and a 180-second minimum residence after a verified move. Close comparisons respect the configured survey interval. The fixed 30-second retry gate covers recent failed attempts and non-executor deferrals; completed or aborted executor operations return to the normal survey interval. These are not promises of a channel change every fixed number of seconds: coverage, service verification, radar clearance and eligibility also matter.

Switch methods default to Auto, which resolves driver support, known WRT execution behavior and recent CSA failures. Explicit methods remain fixed for planned moves; unsupported choices block their corresponding moves. Emergency restoration can still restart the affected radio. Configured/effective methods, reasons and timed destination exclusions are reported in the UI. See the [settings audit](SETTINGS.md) for every setting's scope and removed controls. Strong preferred-radio steering requires a fresh qualifying signal measurement and never silently becomes a gentle request.

## DFS and radar memory

DFS management requires matching global and per-radio ETSI regions. In preparation mode, an empty compatible 5 GHz helper temporarily listens on the target. Another radio is preferred; the target radio itself can act as helper if empty and eligible. Other radios remain available to serve clients. Handoff may empty the helper; preparation does not forcibly restart an occupied helper. Its original AP is restored before the serving radio moves. Clearance must be confirmed on the target radio too; no assumption is made that all drivers share it.

A helper narrower than the proposed block can prepare its uncleared pieces sequentially. Each piece is checked on both PHYs and the helper is restored between checks. The original wide target remains pending; it cannot move until every required member is cleared. Thus an 80 MHz helper can prepare a 160 MHz destination without claiming 160 MHz hardware support.

HT40 setup retains hostapd coexistence checks (`noscan=0`) and periodic overlapping-BSS checks. If setup retains 20 MHz on a member of the requested block, service must still verify. The rejected HT40 placement is remembered for the configured evidence-freshness period, with an explicit reason; it is not mislabeled as failed client connectivity. A verified HT40 checkpoint preserves its secondary direction.

Capability interpretation follows [Linux HT/VHT definitions](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/include/linux/ieee80211.h), regulatory range handling follows [cfg80211 AUTO_BW behavior](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/net/wireless/reg.c), and hostapd performs [HT40 coexistence and VHT configuration checks](https://github.com/vanhoefm/hostap-wpa3/blob/master/hostapd/hostapd.conf). Kernel and hostapd enforcement remains authoritative.

`radar.uc` remembers at most 128 recent incidents independently of optional graph history. Each detection's weight halves over the configured half-life (default one hour). Overlapping incidents accumulate, capped at a risk of one. This affects planning and the fading channel bands in history; it is not radar probability. Kernel non-occupancy periods and CAC requirements always remain authoritative.

## History, persistence and privacy

`history.uc` records channel residence, five-minute traffic summaries, compact spectrum surveys and recent actions. They share the selected retention window of up to 200 hours. The graph's Show selector limits the visible window; it does not change stored retention. Recent actions have no separate count cap. The daemon's 30-entry operational event list is distinct from retained history.

Recent history is bounded to 1 MiB in RAM. `history-storage.uc` offloads immutable batches every six hours during normal operation, keeping the most recent hour cached in RAM. Batches retain original summary detail; no new two-hour aggregation is performed. Flash has no fixed archive-size cap, but writes must leave at least 4 MiB free. Heavy activity or failed offloads can fill RAM and discard oldest buffered records. Expired archive batches are deleted at scheduled maintenance rather than rewritten on each refresh.

Ordinary service restarts reuse same-boot RAM history without forcing flash writes. The orderly shutdown hook saves pending history and radar memory once after the daemon stops. Unexpected power loss loses unsaved data. Disabling recording pauses new records; clearing history removes graph data and actions without erasing radar memory. Only current immutable batch archives are read. Legacy `history.json` archives and two-hour aggregation are no longer supported.

Verified channel settings and radar memory also use periodic six-hour persistence. Radar memory has a shutdown checkpoint; ordinary service restarts recover its same-boot RAM events. Archived graph history omits SSIDs, passwords, browsing activity and client identities; event reasons redact MAC addresses. This is not a claim that every live status file is anonymous. Radio Preference status identifies clients for its UI, client-specific recovery uses a private same-boot RAM file, and the temporary recovery journal can contain the local AP configuration and credentials. Those runtime files, real UCI network/wireless configuration, router backups and browser storage are not inputs to the package builder and are not shipped. Review and redact diagnostics before publishing them.

## Supported scope

Live validation is specific to the WRT3200ACM and its single-AP HT/VHT radios. HE/EHT and MLO are outside current support. Multi-interface arrangements are not validated; restart and preparation paths require a compatible single BSS. Hardware-specific defaults and the WRT3200ACM/WRT32X country helper are intentional compatibility code, not a second planning algorithm.

Fresh packaged configurations enable automatic operation, active-where-permitted paced scanning and DFS preparation. If `enabled` is missing, the daemon does not start. Missing `mode`, `scan_type` or `dfs_mode` fall back to observation, passive scanning and DFS off respectively. Existing current-format administrator settings are preserved; obsolete `move_clients` settings are not interpreted.


## Standard spectrum display

The history axis selects complete standard WLAN band plans from nl80211 PHY band declarations, including disabled radios and disabled frequencies. Current channels, manual pools, regulatory restrictions, DFS state and historical records never select or trim an axis band. This display mapping does not change planning or legal transmission checks.

The fixed plans show 2.4 GHz channels 1-14, 5 GHz through channel 177 (including the older J52 lower edge), and 6 GHz through channel 233 when declared. The 6 GHz display table does not add 6 GHz coordination support. Sources: [HPE Aruba channel sets through 177](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/CLI-Bank/Content/instant/sh-ap-allwd-cha.htm), [Cisco J52 channel definitions](https://www.cisco.com/en/US/docs/switches/lan/catalyst3850/software/release/3.2_0_se/multibook/configuration_guide/b_multibook_config_guide_wireless_3850_chapter_010010.pdf), and [Sophos band channel tables](https://docs.sophos.com/nsg/wifi/help/en-us/GettingStarted/ChannelSelectionGuide/index.html). These are WLAN band plans, not the separate 4.9 GHz public-safety or 60 GHz plans.

The 5330-5490 MHz gap between channels 64 and 100 and the inter-band gaps collapse to 16 display units, marked `//`. The small 5730-5735 MHz separation remains at its true 5 MHz scale. MHz distances stay proportional inside each section. Radio outlines, survey cells and radar bands share this transform and are clipped at section boundaries, preventing shading across a break. Radio colours, hatching, white edging and traffic charts are retained. Survey and radar cells disable Chart.js rectangle inflation, avoiding dark seams where translucent cells meet. The page keeps compact legends and captions; additional interpretation is available on hover. Chart.js parses the rectangular outline paths so their backwards-in-time closing edges are not mistaken for sorted time-series points.

Storage diagnostics are collapsed by default and retain their open state across refreshes. Survey cells extend each observed value until the next measurement, without an artificial expiry; their tooltips expose measurement time. This display persistence does not extend the planner's freshness window.

History UI reads use the authenticated LuCI CGI download transport (`fs.read_direct`), because RAM and archive batches can exceed the ordinary ubus file-read limit. The ACL grants CGI download access in addition to the existing path-specific file read permissions; neither is bypassed. Immutable batches remain cached in the browser; failures are shown above the graphs rather than looking like valid gaps.
