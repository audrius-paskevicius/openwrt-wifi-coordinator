# Current architecture

The daemon in `channel-coordinator/files/main.uc` owns surveying, placement, execution and preferred-radio steering in one event loop. `controller.uc` contains shared movement cost, reservations and bounded return accounting; `steering.uc` contains the preference policy. Hardware calls remain injected into survey and executor tests. One channel operation runs at a time; bounded preference reconnection watches reserve their affected radios. Its serving radio, helper, handoff source/destination and client-recovery destination are reserved in memory. Scans and preference on unrelated radios continue. Survey failures cancel/retry only the survey; execution failures enter the executor's recovery path. Shared inventory failures remain a common safety concern. There is no second steering daemon or cross-process lease.

## Surveying

`survey.uc` collects broad sweeps, visiting the preferred radio's band first. A completed band becomes usable before the other band finishes. Fresh band snapshots are combined; timestamps conservatively use the start of the oldest retained sweep. Every candidate spectrum still needs complete coverage, so partial scans cannot make unseen spectrum look empty. Paced mode visits one frequency at a time and does not require traffic silence. It prefers empty radios; among occupied radios it estimates interrupted client capacity from negotiated rates, traffic and measured scan duration. It waits for the least-cost scanner rather than borrowing a costlier radio during its serving gap. A ready frequency on another band can be scanned meanwhile.

Defaults are a 10% scan-duty budget and at least three seconds of serving time between occupied-radio visits. Active probes are used only where allowed; DFS and other restricted frequencies use passive listening. Empty-only and sustained-quiet modes remain supported. The latter uses the same traffic threshold and quiet duration as quiet-only channel changes.

Only radios with complete, fresh coverage of their candidate spectrum may move. An incomplete other band does not block a fully measured band. Missing frequencies are unknown, not clear spectrum. On 2.4 GHz, scans also cover neighboring frequencies whose networks can overlap allowed candidate channels.

## Planning and evidence

`policy.uc` supplies geometry, regulatory checks and the neighbor-pressure calculation shared with history graphs. `planner.uc` is the single planning implementation. It evaluates driver-advertised HT/VHT widths (20/40 MHz on 2.4 GHz and 20/40/80/160 MHz on 5 GHz), with at most four radios and a bounded search of 100,000 nodes. If the bound is exceeded, the decision holds the current layout rather than executing a partially searched result. HT40 considers both secondary-channel directions. VHT160 uses standard contiguous blocks, not sliding centers or 80+80. AUTO_BW can join contiguous regulatory ranges while preserving per-member width, power and DFS restrictions.

The `channels` setting defaults to `auto`. Each inventory refresh resolves it from the radio's current nl80211 frequency capabilities, confined to its active band. Disabled frequencies and non-DFS frequencies prohibiting AP initiation are excluded. DFS frequencies remain subject to DFS mode, kernel state and clearance checks. An explicit numeric list restricts primary channels, independently of width: listing 36 can permit an 80 MHz block with primary 36 without also listing 40, 44 and 48. Every member frequency must still pass driver, regulatory and DFS checks. No fixed 1–11 or 5 GHz channel list limits automatic discovery. The controller still supports the HT/VHT bands described above, not 6 GHz.

For each 20 MHz member of a candidate block, estimated congestion is the larger of scaled neighbor pressure and available external busy-airtime evidence, capped at 100. The candidate's occupancy is their mean. Busy counters on channels carrying our clients are excluded from planning because they include our own traffic. Missing counters do not add invented congestion; the neighbor estimate remains available.

The service estimate follows nested primary 20/40/80/160 MHz blocks. For each block, neighbour pressure sums each BSS's strongest overlap with any member once, so one wide BSS is not counted repeatedly. The largest available external busy counter can increase this estimate. These are interference proxies, not independent busy probabilities. Incremental bandwidth is weighted by the resulting free fraction: `20*f20 + 20*f40 + 40*f80 + 80*f160`, stopping at the candidate width. This models narrower transmissions when secondary spectrum is busy and makes a clean primary useful to narrow clients too. Mean occupancy remains a separate display value, not the ranking objective.

Radar risk discounts that usable bandwidth by `1 - 0.75*risk`. Service index is `100 * usable_MHz * advertised_bidirectional_streams / reference_MHz`, where the fixed reference is 40 MHz for 2.4 GHz and 160 MHz for 5 GHz. HT and VHT MCS capabilities supply stream counts; absent or undecoded capabilities conservatively use one. The reference is shared within a band, so a capable main radio and a single-stream spare are not normalized into equals. Index values can exceed 100. Internal cost is `100 - service` and can be negative.

This is a stable hardware-headroom objective, independent of current client counts, current offered traffic and transient negotiated rates. It preserves room for future clients; it does not assume that each client can use all streams or the full configured width. Existing clients still constrain execution and connectivity recovery. No client capability database or new dependency is introduced. Explicit unsupported-CSA responses are remembered in a small private capability file keyed to the board, firmware and kernel identity; a change of that identity resets the learned limitation. The model cannot predict individual signal quality, hidden nodes, actual throughput or future demand. Its bonded-channel structure follows the primary/secondary behavior described in [Cisco's RRM white paper](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/wireless/controller/technotes/8-3/b_RRM_White_Paper/dca.html); the service score is this project's heuristic, not Cisco's algorithm.

Joint layouts rank by least self-overlap when separation is enabled, then greatest total service after movement cost. Width and legal transmit power break equal-score ties. If separation is disabled, overlap instead competes through the configured cost penalty. There is no width-first ranking, congestion cutoff for narrowing or radar-risk cliff. Primary frequency is part of placement identity. The planner evaluates all eligible primaries, retaining the best primary per footprint plus the incumbent for joint search. Movement cost uses the existing required-improvement setting as its unit: an empty move costs 0.25 units. For `n` affected clients, an occupied CSA starts at `0.5 + 0.5*n` units and a restart costs `1 + 2*n`. Recent observed departure fractions interpolate CSA cost toward restart cost. Changing primary within the same block doubles that cost. These are admission preferences, not estimated seconds or Mbps. The hardware service objective remains independent of client mix; current occupancy affects only the price of reaching a placement. Width tie-breaking sums each width divided by that radio's maximum configured/current width; it has no authority to override a service difference. Recovery exclusions still cover the whole failed footprint, so changing primary cannot evade them.

Movement cost includes the complete route, including repeated staging exposure and the clients on the helper selected by the executor. An empty helper adds 0.25 units; an occupied helper adds restart cost. Self-preparation counts its clients once at restart cost. Among possible staging routes, lower movement cost wins before fewer steps. Current association counts are estimates for future steps; each step is replanned before execution. Temporary residence, retry and return-observation holds retain visible alternatives but fix the held radio at its current placement in the joint search. Hard eligibility and recovery restrictions still filter candidates.

CSA learning uses distinct clients observed leaving during ordinary completed CSA verification, divided by the initial source count. A new observation has one-quarter weight after the first. It excludes external recovery, repair and reconnect operations, and is not proof that CSA caused each departure. Same-boot memory expires after one day without an observation or a hardware/configuration-intent change; temporary method resolution does not reset it. Radar evacuation and failed-service recovery do not wait for the ordinary improvement margin.

The search orders candidates by direct cost and prunes branches using an optimistic remaining-service and minimum-movement bound. The bound does not replace final route costing or override separation priority.

Transitions may use one neutral staging move; with separation enabled, no intermediate move may increase overlap. The next step is reevaluated after each completed operation. Among executable moves, the radio with the oldest last attempt/completed move goes first, independent of its name or enumeration order. This prevents an unrelated radio with repeated width fallbacks from starving a multi-step placement.

Evidence uncertainty starts at one point with complete measured airtime, otherwise three. Changes in usable capacity between surveys can raise it to 15; a changed scanner imposes a floor of six. It is converted to service-index units using the radio's maximum width and advertised streams. Supporting-survey counts are explanatory; repetition alone does not shrink uncertainty. Net benefit, after movement cost, must exceed both the configured minimum and current comparison uncertainty. Improved separation can bypass that margin. Every prepared step reruns the joint planner against current observations, client occupancy and legal placements before disruption. Its own retry gate is bypassed for this check; configuration, coverage, compatibility and regulatory restrictions remain. An old multi-step plan has no privileged admission.

The last completed comparison is retained in the RAM state file across surveys, moves and service restarts within the same boot. It is explanatory evidence, never an execution queue. Sampling marks placement changes; the UI shows evaluation time, age and current progress, and labels all placement, pool and exclusion data as belonging to that snapshot. A completed evaluation replaces it. The dedicated Comparison tab before History displays this assessment directly; Channels contains operating status; Settings owns configuration. Unchanged comparison tables retain their DOM while live status updates. Failed refreshes retain the last results with a warning, and comparisons older than the configured survey freshness limit are labelled.

The comparison table includes the direct movement cost for each alternative; the summary includes complete-route cost and estimated client exposure. The comparison table shows individual alternatives sorted by sharing with peer placements at comparison time, then decreasing service index. Estimated usable width is before stream weighting but after the radar discount; primary/block congestion shows per-primary and mean block estimates. These are not percentages of measured airtime. The table is not the joint-plan ranking: the joint plan can also move peers.

## Execution, clients and timing

`executor.uc` changes one radio at a time. It rechecks configuration, regulatory eligibility and peer overlap immediately before execution. It uses CSA when supported. A restart first attempts a same-band, same-network handoff; if none exists, it skips that wait. With the shared reconnect fallback enabled, it can restart the selected single-AP radio when clients remain. Protected clients and explicit quiet-only/empty-only policies still restrict restart operations.

Handoff defaults to 90 seconds, progressing from gentle requests to stronger requests and then a bounded disconnect if decisive escalation is enabled. Temporary return bans are cleared before the reconnect fallback. Recovery uses reachable private-LAN ping targets, bidirectional data counters and associations. Missing clients get a 60-second reconnection grace period before persistent-failure handling; unprobeable clients require a longer association observation. Association alone does not establish working internet. Confirmed radio failures trigger rollback and six-hour destination exclusions. Client checks use the scoped recovery policy below. Interrupted operations restore their recovery journal, then observe enabled, previously known APs for 30 seconds of stable channel state before automatically resuming. Disabled APs do not block this check. No client traffic or roaming is required, and the observation itself does not restart radios. Recovery is blocked while a journal remains, an AP is missing or inconsistent, or a previously recorded per-radio failure remains unresolved. After 780 seconds (the fixed internal `cac_timeout` of 720 plus 60), an unsuccessful check schedules another assessment with a 60/120/240/300-second capped backoff. Journal restoration failures are retried on the same schedule. A restart or expired timer never clears a failed radio by itself. Each failed or unavailable radio is observed separately: its AP must be enabled, consistent and legal, stay on the same placement with the same authorized clients for 60 seconds, and show fresh bidirectional traffic or successful probes for every currently authorized client. Empty APs need the same stable observation. An unsuccessful assessment lasts at least 180 seconds (or `verify_unprobed`, if greater), then backs off up to 300 seconds and tries again. These checks do not move or restart a radio, and recovering radios are excluded from scans. Departed clients do not permanently block reassessment; timed destination and client compatibility restrictions remain. If service never becomes verifiable, the radio remains visibly deferred with recurring checks rather than falsely claiming recovery. This checks AP readiness, not end-to-end internet access.

When channel setup retains a narrower member block, on either band, the observed width constraint applies across that radio's primary channels. The first pause uses `max_age` (normally 15 minutes); repeated refusals double it up to six hours. Attempt memory survives pause expiry, resets after a day without a refusal or a hardware/configuration-intent change, and clears after verified operation at the requested width. Moving to another primary does not immediately retry the same unavailable width. The UI reports the active reason and remaining time; configured widths and hardware capabilities remain unchanged. The comparison retains only the constraint active when evaluated.

Client recovery distinguishes a failed radio, a working AP with an affected client, and inconclusive evidence. Fresh per-AP bidirectional data or a successful probe with an unambiguous association establishes positive service evidence. An association alone cannot verify an alternate AP. When other clients demonstrate that the destination works, only affected clients receive bounded handoff requests to a compatible same-band AP with matching security and LAN. Existing escalation, signal checks, protected-client rules and the shared five-minute forced-disconnect budget apply. If alternate service verifies, the new placement remains available to healthy clients.

A successful selective recovery records a six-hour client/radio/placement restriction in a private, same-boot RAM file, capped at 256 entries. Radio Preference and channel handoffs avoid recommending that combination; voluntary association is still allowed. The coordinator tries a verified preflight handoff before reusing it for that client. Without a permitted, verified fallback, the placement is deferred for six hours. Records expire automatically and are never archived to flash; history contains only anonymous counts and outcomes.

A failed client check does not by itself disable CSA. A working AP without a verified client fallback receives a six-hour compatibility deferral. Inconclusive checks initially defer the placement for one evidence-freshness interval (`max_age`, normally 15 minutes); positive recovery of the affected client on the previous placement promotes this to six hours. Recovery never bounces from the restored original placement back to the failed destination. If the restored AP demonstrably serves other clients and only unprotected, absent clients remain unconfirmed, planning pauses for one freshness interval rather than marking the radio permanently failed. Otherwise the failed radio enters recurring service reassessment. No check here establishes end-to-end internet performance.

Default timing is 60 seconds between completed surveys and a 180-second minimum residence after a verified move. Close comparisons respect the configured survey interval. The fixed 30-second retry gate covers recent failed attempts and non-executor deferrals; completed or aborted executor operations return to the normal survey interval. These are not promises of a channel change every fixed number of seconds: coverage, service verification, radar clearance and eligibility also matter.

A rejected CSA request retains its client baseline and checks actual service. An explicit unsupported/not-implemented response is remembered for Auto until the board, firmware or kernel identity changes; explicit administrator method choices are preserved. Other CSA failures retain their bounded six-hour fallback. Temporary observation errors retain the operation for at most `verify_unprobed + 60` consecutive seconds before recurring reassessment. Journal restoration is verified. An expired stability watch with no persistently missing clients keeps the current ready AP and records an unconfirmed result, instead of restarting it. Real persistent loss still starts recovery. Recovery has one bounded repair attempt and never bounces back to a failed destination; the driver remains responsible for immediate radar evacuation. Healthy service elsewhere and preferred-radio return are reported separately.

Switch methods default to Auto, which resolves driver support, known WRT execution behavior and recent CSA failures. Explicit methods remain fixed for planned moves; unsupported choices block their corresponding moves. Emergency restoration can still restart the affected radio. Configured/effective methods, reasons and timed destination exclusions are reported in the UI. See the [settings audit](SETTINGS.md) for every setting's scope and removed controls.

Preferred-radio steering and channel/radar handoffs use the shared `handoff.uc`
policy and `channel_coordinator.main.handoff_escalation` setting. Gentle sends a
recommendation; Strong adds a disassociation warning after one third of the
handoff window; Decisive may disconnect remaining eligible clients after two thirds.
The default window is 90 seconds. Requests preserve the complete neighbour report,
replace its preference subelement, and use no autonomous AP disconnect timer.
The controller enforces the optional decisive step with a 20-second return ban.

Measurement requests accompany the first gentle preference request instead of
holding it for a minute. Missing reports never suppress the Strong warning. A
fresh weak target report blocks both paths. Force requires a qualifying target
report, or strong source signal on the same band; source-only inference cannot
force an unmeasured 2.4 GHz client onto 5 GHz. A client without 802.11v support
cannot receive warnings, but Decisive can still act if these eligibility checks pass.
Protected clients remain excluded.

Both paths share per-client/destination attempt budgets (normally a 300-second
interval and three attempts) and the existing one-force-per-client/five-minute
budget. Attempts include the whole escalation sequence, not each stage. The
same-boot attempt ledger is capped at 256 entries and expires automatically.
The old separate preference `mode` and `min_rssi` settings are retired. Migration
preserves an existing handoff policy; if absent, it maps old Advisory to Gentle
and otherwise chooses Strong. The shared measured-signal minimum retains the
stricter of the previous two thresholds.

Completion remains purpose-specific. Radar preparation requires the source radio
empty and its tracked clients authorized elsewhere. Preference observes arrival
on the preferred AP; association alone is not proof of working internet. It never
restarts an AP to force preference. An unsuccessful attempt releases the temporary
ban and permits service on the original AP. During the bounded forced-reconnection
watch, the source and target are reserved against ordinary moves and scans. Channel
operations can preempt other preference stages; source/target placement changes,
weak target evidence, protection changes and deadline expiry cancel an episode.
Shutdown clears outstanding bans when possible; their 20-second limit also bounds
an abrupt process loss. Restart never replays a pending warning or disconnect.

After a successful operation on the preferred radio, original/late-arriving clients
get a 120-second return observation and at most one extra attempt using the same
escalation policy. It can bypass the ordinary attempt budget, but not protection,
compatibility, signal checks or the forced-disconnect budget. Returned, elsewhere,
absent and declined counts remain separate; helper-only clients are excluded.
The preferred radio stays available during that bounded return observation.
Other radios continue normally, and kernel radar evacuation takes priority.
Expiry releases the reservation without declaring a placement failure.

## DFS and radar memory

DFS management requires matching, known global and per-radio DFS regions (FCC, ETSI or Japan), following the configured country and live kernel rules. Private driver debug fields do not impose an additional eligibility veto. In preparation mode, an empty compatible 5 GHz helper temporarily listens on the target. Another radio is preferred; the target radio itself can act as helper if empty and eligible. Other radios remain available to serve clients. Handoff may empty the helper; preparation does not forcibly restart an occupied helper. Its original AP is restored before the serving radio moves. Clearance must be confirmed on the target radio too; no assumption is made that all drivers share it.

A helper narrower than the proposed block can prepare its uncleared pieces sequentially. Each piece is checked on both PHYs and the helper is restored between checks. The original wide target remains pending; it cannot move until every required member is cleared. Thus an 80 MHz helper can prepare a 160 MHz destination without claiming 160 MHz hardware support.

HT40 setup retains hostapd coexistence checks (`noscan=0`) and periodic overlapping-BSS checks. If setup retains 20 MHz on a member of the requested block, service must still verify. The rejected HT40 placement is remembered for the configured evidence-freshness period, with an explicit reason; repeated width refusals also double a radio-wide width retry delay, capped at six hours and reset after a day without an attempt; it is not mislabeled as failed client connectivity. A width refusal alone leaves 20 MHz eligible. If service actually fails at the retained 20 MHz placement, its separate failure restriction also excludes HT40 requests that could fall back to it, including a reversed primary. The planner and executor enforce this until the failure restriction expires; unrelated blocks remain eligible. A verified HT40 checkpoint preserves its secondary direction.

Capability interpretation follows [Linux HT/VHT definitions](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/include/linux/ieee80211.h), regulatory range handling follows [cfg80211 AUTO_BW behavior](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.12/net/wireless/reg.c), and hostapd performs [HT40 coexistence and VHT configuration checks](https://github.com/vanhoefm/hostap-wpa3/blob/master/hostapd/hostapd.conf). Kernel and hostapd enforcement remains authoritative.

`radar.uc` remembers at most 128 recent incidents independently of optional graph history. Each detection's weight halves over the configured half-life (default one hour). Overlapping incidents accumulate, capped at a risk of one. This affects planning; it is not radar probability. Kernel non-occupancy periods and CAC requirements always remain authoritative. After exclusion expiry, fresh coverage, service scoring, residence timing and recovery restrictions still determine whether to revisit a block; reuse is opportunistic, not an immediate scheduled return.

History reconstructs fading radar bands from retained archive/RAM detection events plus current planner memory, using the configured half-life. It does not inherit the planner's 128-incident limit or lose old shading when a planner checkpoint prunes records. Shared PHY reports for the same region/block within five seconds count once. CAC completion and non-occupancy expiry do not create radar bands. Legacy detections use known incident geometry or a unique contemporaneous channel block; ambiguous records show only the reported primary's 20 MHz. The [WRT3200ACM driver investigation](DFS-DRIVER.md) distinguishes observed behavior from unverified firmware questions.

## History, persistence and privacy

The client-count chart adds staggered eight-pixel fins above its solid lines, twelve pixels apart per radio. The 45-degree/vertical slants match the placement hatching and repeat in the legend. Fins are pixel-sized identity marks, not additional clients, and draw after all datasets so overlapping counts remain identifiable. All three charts use an aligned 80-pixel value-axis gutter. The Recent actions ordering note is attached to its title.

All three history charts share adaptive, aligned 24-hour time ticks in the browser's local timezone. No separate timezone caption is displayed. Views shorter than 24 hours, or wholly within one local calendar day, show only `HH:mm` ticks. Dates appear on a separate tick line only when the view spans at least 24 hours and crosses local midnight. Nothing is labelled beneath the time axis. Tooltips, action records and comparison timestamps use `YYYY-MM-DD HH:mm:ss`. Repeated tick labels during the autumn clock change include their UTC offset. Stored epoch timestamps are unchanged.

Radar shading uses saturated amber with 100% intensity comparable to the grey congestion scale. Opacity falls linearly with remembered strength, fading toward white in the legend and revealing the existing survey background in the graph. This does not change the radar-memory half-life or planner scores. The amber legend and graph share the same colour function. Both legend bars are 180 pixels wide with five evenly spaced numeric ticks. Radar memory runs from 100% to 0%, ending at pure white; its configured decay times appear in the hover explanation. Congestion retains its distinct 0-100 estimate scale, without percent signs. The two scales still represent different quantities: congestion estimates and remembered radar avoidance strength.

Action records link preparation, handoff, verification and return by an operation ID. Disconnect events count across the whole operation, including preparation. Delayed departures of evacuated clients do not reset verification of clients already served elsewhere; forced handoffs have a separate counter.

`history.uc` records channel residence, constant-count client intervals, five-minute traffic summaries, compact spectrum surveys and recent actions. They share the selected retention window of up to 200 hours. The graph's Show selector limits the visible window; it does not change stored retention. Client counts are sampled with the five-second recorder, coalesced while unchanged, and separated by gaps over 15 seconds or a new boot. Their graph uses the traffic chart palette, dimensions and time axis, with whole-number counts and no interpolation across missing observations. The new optional `clients` array shares RAM bounds, retention, six-hour archives and exports. Older history stays readable without inventing counts from peaks. Recent actions have no separate count cap. The daemon's 30-entry operational event list is distinct from retained history.

Recent history is bounded to 1 MiB in RAM. `history-storage.uc` offloads immutable batches every six hours during normal operation, keeping the most recent hour cached in RAM. Batches retain original summary detail; no new two-hour aggregation is performed. Flash has no fixed archive-size cap, but writes must leave at least 4 MiB free. Heavy activity or failed offloads can fill RAM and discard oldest buffered records. Expired archive batches are deleted at scheduled maintenance rather than rewritten on each refresh.

Ordinary service restarts reuse same-boot RAM history without forcing flash writes. The orderly shutdown hook saves pending history and radar memory once after the daemon stops. Unexpected power loss loses unsaved data. Disabling recording pauses new records; clearing history removes recorded placements, client counts, traffic, surveys and actions without erasing planner radar memory. That memory can still produce radar shading after clearing. Only current immutable batch archives are read. Legacy `history.json` archives and two-hour aggregation are no longer supported.

Verified channel settings and radar memory also use periodic six-hour persistence. Radar memory has a shutdown checkpoint; ordinary service restarts recover its same-boot RAM events. Archived graph history omits SSIDs, passwords, browsing activity and client identities; event reasons redact MAC addresses. This is not a claim that every live status file is anonymous. Radio Preference status identifies clients for its UI, client-specific recovery uses a private same-boot RAM file, and the temporary recovery journal can contain the local AP configuration and credentials. Those runtime files, real UCI network/wireless configuration, router backups and browser storage are not inputs to the package builder and are not shipped. Review and redact diagnostics before publishing them.

## Supported scope

Live validation is specific to the WRT3200ACM and its single-AP HT/VHT radios. HE/EHT and MLO are outside current support. Multi-interface arrangements are not validated; restart and preparation paths require a compatible single BSS. Hardware-specific defaults and the WRT3200ACM/WRT32X country helper are intentional compatibility code, not a second planning algorithm.

Fresh packaged configurations enable automatic operation, active-where-permitted paced scanning and DFS preparation. If `enabled` is missing, the daemon does not start. Missing `mode`, `scan_type` or `dfs_mode` fall back to observation, passive scanning and DFS off respectively. Existing current-format administrator settings are preserved; obsolete `move_clients` settings are not interpreted.


## Standard spectrum display

The history axis selects complete standard WLAN band plans from nl80211 PHY band declarations, including disabled radios and disabled frequencies. Current channels, manual pools, regulatory restrictions, DFS state and historical records never select or trim an axis band. This display mapping does not change planning or legal transmission checks.

The fixed plans show 2.4 GHz channels 1-14, 5 GHz through channel 177 (including the older J52 lower edge), and 6 GHz through channel 233 when declared. The 6 GHz display table does not add 6 GHz coordination support. Sources: [HPE Aruba channel sets through 177](https://arubanetworking.hpe.com/techdocs/CLI-Bank/Content/instant/sh-ap-allwd-cha.htm), [Cisco J52 channel definitions](https://www.cisco.com/en/US/docs/switches/lan/catalyst3850/software/release/3.2_0_se/multibook/configuration_guide/b_multibook_config_guide_wireless_3850_chapter_010010.pdf), and [Sophos band channel tables](https://docs.sophos.com/nsg/wifi/help/en-us/GettingStarted/ChannelSelectionGuide/index.html). These are WLAN band plans, not the separate 4.9 GHz public-safety or 60 GHz plans.

The 5330-5490 MHz gap between channels 64 and 100 and the inter-band gaps collapse to 16 display units, marked `//`. The small 5730-5735 MHz separation remains at its true 5 MHz scale. MHz distances stay proportional inside each section. Radio outlines, survey cells and radar bands share this transform and are clipped at section boundaries, preventing shading across a break. Radio colours, hatching, white edging and traffic charts are retained. Survey and radar cells disable Chart.js rectangle inflation, avoiding dark seams where translucent cells meet. The page keeps compact legends; graph explanations are attached to each title and available on hover or activation by keyboard or tap. Chart.js parses the rectangular outline paths so their backwards-in-time closing edges are not mistaken for sorted time-series points.

Storage diagnostics, retention controls and downloads are on the separate Storage & export tab. History contains graphs, summaries and recent actions, with no Save buttons. Storage diagnostics are collapsed by default and retain their open state across refreshes. Survey cells extend each observed value until the next measurement, without an artificial expiry; their tooltips expose measurement time. This display persistence does not extend the planner's freshness window.

History UI reads use the authenticated LuCI CGI download transport (`fs.read_direct`), because RAM and archive batches can exceed the ordinary ubus file-read limit. The ACL grants CGI download access in addition to the existing path-specific file read permissions; neither is bypassed. Immutable batches remain cached in the browser. Interrupted requests get two retries after 250 and 750 milliseconds; overlapping refreshes share one request sequence. A failed directory listing preserves the archive cache. Failed recent-history or controller reads preserve their last successful browser snapshots, with a refresh warning until recovery. Permission errors and corrupt data remain explicit errors. Successful listings prune removed batches, and confirmed missing recent-history files clear their cached snapshots. This adds no router flash writes or channel-policy changes.

History exports use a read-only ucode CGI and `history-export.uc`, with the existing LuCI session and path-specific read permissions. They stream JSON or dataset-specific CSV directly to the download manager, without a temporary export file, full-archive buffer or flash writes. One batch is parsed at a time; one dataset's deduplication index grows with record count. The request fixes its RAM snapshot, cutoff and archive listing. Missing sources are reported in a final completeness result. See [export schema and snapshot semantics](EXPORT.md).

## Process supervision

Both services use procd respawn with a 30-second delay and no retry-count limit. Steering preparation runs inside the supervised process, so a transient preparation failure is retried too. Disabled services remain disabled; invalid configuration or competing controllers are still reported, not silently overridden. This does not guarantee recovery from hardware faults or certify years of unattended uptime. The retry-limit semantics follow [OpenWrt procd](https://lxr.openwrt.org/source/procd/service/instance.c).

## Configuration presentation

The Settings view chains the existing `channel_coordinator` and `radio_prefer`
UCI namespaces in one native LuCI map. Preference fields explicitly bind to their
original namespace and section; the independent enable switches do not alias one
another. Nested radio sections preserve per-radio keys. Conditional controls use
retention, so selecting another policy does not delete previously configured
values. No schema migration or algorithm-policy change accompanies this layout.
The steering counter now displays the current shared attempt ledger rather than
the cached count at the most recent preference attempt. See the
[configuration audit](SETTINGS-AUDIT.md) for the algorithm/UI boundary.
