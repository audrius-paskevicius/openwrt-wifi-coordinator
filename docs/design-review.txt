Wi-Fi Coordinator: complete algorithm assessment
6 November 2026; baseline 0.5.4-r4

Conclusion
The implementation has useful hardware integration and measurement tools, but
its control policy is an accumulation of local rules. Lowering individual
thresholds cannot make it consistently effective. Replace the central decision
and execution flow while retaining tested hardware adapters, geometry, history
storage and packaging. Do not add another collection of fast-path exceptions.

Scope and evidence
Reviewed survey scheduling, observation parsing, scoring, width selection,
joint placement, next-step selection, confirmation, cooldown, handoff, DFS,
verification, recovery, Radio Preference/usteer ownership, history and UI.
Sources: channel-coordinator/files/main.uc, policy.uc, history.uc,
history-storage.uc; radio-prefer/files/main.uc and coordination.uc.
The assessment separates code findings from live evidence. It does not claim
that a lighter survey background guarantees greater client throughput.

1. Objective and scoring
The current score combines beacon-derived neighbour pressure, available busy
airtime, a missing-data cost, width-derived capacity and allowed-power cost.
These are useful inputs but do not have a calibrated common meaning. A beacon
scan estimates nearby competition; it does not measure the fraction of airtime
available to our clients. The formula width * (1 - occupancy) is a ranking proxy,
not measured goodput. It also does not account for each client's spatial streams,
signal quality or demand. Equal radio scores do not mean equal client capacity.

The grey graph and planner share geometry, which is good. However, uncertainty
should describe confidence in a comparison, not silently act as congestion.
Keep measured airtime, neighbour pressure and client performance distinguishable.
Use clearer spectrum and usable width as the practical selection objective;
validate the tradeoff with bounded client tests, without routine speed tests.
Lower permitted power should be a coverage consideration, not an arbitrary
substitute for observed client quality.

2. Width and joint coordination
The joint planner correctly prioritises separation, but preferred width is
computed against other radios' CURRENT locations before joint placements are
searched. It can therefore undervalue a wider arrangement unlocked by moving
another radio. The final layout search and executable-step search also disagree:
each intermediate move must independently improve its score. This can prevent a
useful rearrangement that needs a neutral staging step.

Evaluate channels and widths together for the final layout, then construct a
feasible transition sequence. A staging step is justified by a better final plan
and a bounded connectivity cost, not by pretending the staging step itself wins.
Keep all radios in the same policy. Driver capabilities belong in adapters.
Preserve the user's preference for wide channels when suitable spectrum exists;
narrowing is a fallback supported by better expected service, not a reaction to
any visible neighbour. Re-evaluate after each real transition before continuing.

3. Decision stability
The old destination-only switching penalty duplicated the improvement margin;
0.5.4-r4 removes it. Other overlapping brakes remain: matching surveys, proposal
tolerance, per-radio attempt/move cooldown, cross-radio settling, traffic silence,
handoff waits and verification windows. Some serve different purposes, but they
are not organised around one decision. A failed preparation attempt currently
locks a radio for the same duration as a completed move.

Use one explicit admission rule for a move: sufficient benefit given the quality
of the evidence. Strong evidence can justify action immediately; an ambiguous
comparison requests additional measurements. Do not make every improvement wait
for the same number of complete sweeps. Keep anti-reversal hysteresis separate
from short failure retries. Every deferral must identify its concrete blocker and
the observation, event or deadline that will release it.

4. Surveys
Paced single-frequency scans, empty-radio preference and complete-band coverage
are valuable. A fixed traffic ceiling can still starve measurements under steady
use. Full sweeps are repeated even when the unresolved decision concerns only a
small number of blocks. Whole-sweep leases pause Radio Preference, so faster
scanning alone can also reduce steering opportunities.

Use a bounded scan duty cycle with serving gaps, preferentially on a spare radio.
Maintain broad coverage on a schedule; prioritise stale or decision-relevant
blocks between broad sweeps. Account for scan duration and client health rather
than requiring an indefinitely quiet network. Missing evidence stays unknown.
Keep per-radio/per-frequency provenance and freshness. Avoid interpreting stronger
reception by a different scanner as a sudden increase in actual channel usage.

5. Execution and clients
Current automatic handoff policy waits for quiet traffic before CSA, or tries to
evacuate clients. CSA and restart-based changes need different execution paths:
live CSA can retain clients, whereas this router's restart path must first empty
the radio. Active traffic should not automatically require kicking clients off
a CSA-capable radio. An explicit administrator quiet-only policy can remain.

One orchestrator should own the operation: reserve a destination/helper, perform
only necessary handoffs, prepare DFS if needed, switch one radio, verify, then
release normal client placement. Never let ordinary steering pull clients back
onto a reserved or preparing radio. usteer can remain the measurement provider.
The existing leases and passive-usteer ownership are useful foundations.

Radio Preference is currently a separate fixed-preferred-radio loop, not a
maximum-client-throughput optimiser. Its three-attempt limit can persist until
the client reaches the preferred radio or disappears. Expiring evidence and
outcome-based retry state are more appropriate than an indefinite attempt limit.
Do not treat radio0 as universally best solely because it is named radio0.
The live PC fallback also reached no-measurement-support in Radio Preference.
That is an eligibility dead end, not evidence that its current 2.4 GHz link is
best. The design needs an explicit policy for incomplete client measurements.

6. Recovery and external events
Live evidence at 23:08:15: after radio0 moved from 100/80 to 116/80, the driver
reported radar. hostapd selected 132/80 and performed CAC. The coordinator was
still verifying its requested 116/80 placement, then raised a generic safety
stop despite recording zero persistently missing clients in that event.
The radar listener updates pending radar state during coordinator-managed CAC,
but does not give radar evacuation its own verification/recovery transition.

Treat radar, administrator edits, driver resets and spontaneous channel changes
as explicit events that invalidate the old plan. Accept the kernel's regulatory
decision, reconcile the actual operating state, check service, then replan.
Do not fight an evacuation or return to a channel under a radar prohibition.
This observation does not prove whether the physical radar detection was genuine.

Radar memory is a separate input to planning, not Wi-Fi congestion. Keep a bounded
record of detected incidents: time, affected channel geometry/subchannels,
reporting PHYs and regulatory domain. Distinguish detection from CAC completion,
CAC failure and expiration of a kernel prohibition. The existing history already
records raw radar events, but the current planner has no decaying radar-memory
policy. A blanket six-hour connectivity-failure exclusion is not a replacement.

There are two layers:
- Hard eligibility: obey current kernel DFS state and required clearance. No
  software decay, successful scan or reboot may be used to shorten a prohibition.
- Service reliability: after the kernel permits a block, recent detections still
  make it less attractive than otherwise comparable blocks without detections.
  Decay this preference with elapsed time using one configurable half-life;
  repeated incidents strengthen it, and old isolated incidents gradually cease
  to dominate. The half-life is a policy parameter to validate, not a claim about
  when radar will return. A clear beacon scan does not erase radar history.

Associate risk with affected spectrum, so changing the primary channel or radio
does not evade it. Avoid penalising unrelated spectrum or all DFS channels.
Coalesce duplicate notifications of the same incident forwarded to multiple PHYs:
the 23:08:15 detection at 5580 MHz appeared on PHY0 and PHY2 simultaneously. Retain
both reporters without automatically treating that as two independent incidents.
Apply the memory to every candidate width that overlaps affected subchannels.
Expose this as interruption risk/history, separately from the grey Wi-Fi layer.
Use bounded aggregates and existing batched persistence, not frequent flash writes.
Treat untrusted wall-clock changes conservatively when ageing persisted events.

Integrate this input into the single service-quality objective and event-driven
recovery design. Do not bolt on an unexplained second switching threshold.

Separate outcomes: completed move; postponed move; ignored command; radar
evacuation; failed connectivity with successful recovery; unresolved outage.
Quarantine a proven failing operation/placement without globally disabling
healthy radios. Reserve a global stop for an unresolved condition that actually
prevents safe control. A normal client's departure is not proof a scan harmed it.

7. Verification
Association and even authorization alone can be stale. Router-initiated ping is
also not a universal connection test. During this run the PC failed inbound ping
checks, but its own report showed 3/3 replies from the router and 3/3 from this PC
on radio2/52, with no throughput test or reconnect by the peer.

Use multiple observations: authorization, fresh data movement, established
baseline probes, and client-side checks when available. Preserve distinctions
between disconnected, connected-but-unproven and demonstrably working. Roll back
on sustained evidence of service loss; do not call every failed probe an outage.
Verification should establish connectivity first. Claims of improved speed need
separate performance evidence and comparable workloads.

8. History, persistence and UI
Retain bounded recent RAM history and six-hour immutable flash archive batches.
This is already a reasonable foundation and does not need another storage layer.
Every verified channel move also commits wireless configuration; more agile
movement must account for those writes separately from history offloading.
Prefer batching remembered operating state rather than increasing flash writes
in proportion to short-lived channel experiments.

Expose one concise decision record: current layout, selected layout, expected
benefit, confidence, planned transition and actual blocker/outcome. Keep detailed
measurements available on demand. The graph is evidence, not the decision rule.
Show recorded radar evacuation as radar evacuation, not generic client failure.

Proposed replacement architecture
  Observe -> build one timestamped evidence snapshot.
  Plan -> rank legal joint channel/width layouts and their feasible transitions.
  Decide -> apply one benefit/confidence rule; gather more evidence only if needed.
  Execute -> one operation owner, using capability-specific adapters.
  Verify -> accept, recover, or reconcile an external event; feed outcome back.

This is one control cycle, not five independent controllers. Keep policy functions
pure and execution side effects explicit. Keep the essential operator controls
small: evaluation cadence, responsiveness, permitted client disruption and history
retention. Hardware/regulatory limits are capabilities, not tunable aggression.
Do not implement the redesign as additional exceptions inside the current large
operation function. Extract the observation model, joint planner and event-driven
executor behind stable interfaces, then retire the overlapping rules.

Acceptance criteria before deployment
- A clear legal win produces an executable plan, not an indefinitely pending hint.
- Noisy ties do not repeatedly reverse channels; steady traffic cannot starve scans.
- Joint channel/width and staging cases agree with exhaustive small-model results.
- An occupied CSA radio need not be evacuated solely because it carries traffic.
- A restart-based change never restarts an occupied radio.
- Radar during preparation, switching and verification is handled explicitly.
- Radar memory decays monotonically, strengthens on repeated independent events,
  deduplicates shared-PHY reports and affects overlapping channel widths only.
  It never overrides kernel prohibitions or invents detections from CAC events.
- One deferred attempt does not impose a completed-move cooldown.
- Driver no-op, failed client probe, real outage and recovery have distinct tests.
- A failed operation on one radio does not unnecessarily freeze healthy radios.
- Phone and PC tests check interruption and recovery; throughput comparisons use
  matched conditions. Synthetic tests alone cannot validate firmware behavior.
- Flash writes and retained history remain bounded and observable.

Live follow-up
A subsequent PC-side check again passed 3/3 router and 3/3 server probes,
but the PC had fallen back to radio1/2.4 GHz. Reachability and restoration of
the intended high-performance association are therefore separate outcomes.

Implementation status, 7 November 2026
Implemented in0.6.0-r3: separate observation, planner, radar-memory and executor
modules; joint width/channel search with feasible staging; one admission rule;
measured scan-duty budget; scanner selection by interrupted client capacity;
per-radio scan reservations; live CSA without a quiet
traffic gate; bounded per-radio recovery; event-driven radar reconciliation;
batched remembered channels and radar history; matching Chart.js radar decay.
Radio Preference retry budgets expire and unmeasured recommendations are gentle.
The new tests and live observations are recorded in TESTING.txt.

Scope retained for simplicity: close comparisons repeat a broad paced survey;
there is no targeted partial-refresh cache. The capacity score is a heuristic,
not measured client goodput. Up to one neutral staging move is searched. Driver
behavior and seamless client roaming cannot be established by synthetic tests.
