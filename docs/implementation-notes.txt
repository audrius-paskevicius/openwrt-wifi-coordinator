Wi-Fi Coordinator 0.6.0-r7
Local signed APK for OpenWrt 25.12, developed on Linksys WRT3200ACM.

One package combines channel planning and Radio Preference. usteer supplies
measurements with its competing steering decisions held passive. The LuCI pages
are under Network -> Wi-Fi Coordinator. No SSID, Wi-Fi password or router
password is included. Existing administrator settings are preserved.

The controller follows one observe / plan / execute / verify cycle:
- survey.uc schedules paced, single-frequency scans, preferring empty radios.
  For occupied radios, selection estimates the client link capacity interrupted
  by a visit, using negotiated rates and measured visit duration. It waits for
  the least-cost scanner rather than borrowing a costlier radio during its gap.
  Serving radios receive at least three seconds between visits, increased using
  the measured scan duration to respect a default 10% scan-duty budget. Steady
  traffic does not prevent coverage. Only the scanning radio is reserved from
  normal steering. Full broad sweeps remain the measurement unit; a close
  comparison requests an earlier full repeat, not a targeted partial refresh.
- planner.uc ranks legal joint channel AND width arrangements. Separation comes
  first, then usable width, then estimated capacity. All radios follow the same
  policy. Other radios' old positions do not constrain the final width choice.
  A transition route can include one neutral staging move; every step avoids
  increasing overlap and is reevaluated against fresh observations afterwards.
- An explicit benefit/evidence margin replaces fixed confirmation counts and
  stacked switching thresholds. Clear gains can act after one complete sweep.
  Each eligible destination records consecutive fresh surveys where its cost
  beats that radio's current placement, including surveys where it ranks second.
  A rotating winner or another radio's proposed move does not erase that support.
  New destinations start fresh; changed incumbents, contrary evidence, missing
  candidates and stale comparisons reset it. Counters are bounded at 64 and kept
  only in daemon RAM. Each moving radio uses its own destination's support to
  reduce the uncertainty margin; the minimum benefit and joint feasibility checks
  still apply. This is a heuristic, not a statistical confidence guarantee.
  Missing airtime increases uncertainty rather than inventing congestion.
  Recent radar incidents reduce expected service, separately from Wi-Fi load.
- executor.uc owns handoff, DFS preparation, switching, verification and recovery.
  Live CSA can run with traffic and attached clients. Automatic mode first tries
  a compatible handoff. If clients stay, a shared allow_reconnect policy permits
  a brief restart of only that radio; clients reconnect to the same network.
  With no compatible AP, skip the impossible handoff. This common fallback is
  enabled by default. Explicit protected clients and quiet/empty-only policies
  remain respected. Radar preparation still needs an empty helper radio.
  Width changes use the configured width-specific method.
  Restart transactions keep a private RAM recovery journal. Clients receive a
  60-second reconnection grace, followed by service checks. Persistent failure
  restores the previous live channel and excludes the failed destination for
  six hours. A service interruption also restores the recorded previous channel;
  power loss instead boots the saved UCI configuration. No extra flash writes.
- Verification combines authorization, fresh bidirectional data and probes that
  worked before the move. Unknown service remains identified as unproven. A
  sustained failure initiates bounded recovery on the affected radio. An ignored
  command, failed placement and regulatory evacuation have distinct outcomes.

Default cadence: 60 seconds between completed surveys, earlier repeat after 30
seconds for a close comparison, 180-second minimum residence after a verified
move and 30-second retry delay after a deferred operation. Failed destinations
are excluded for six hours. A failed CSA data path temporarily selects an
radio restart instead, using the shared handoff/reconnect policy. Explicit quiet-only/empty-only policies remain.

The spectrum score uses the same neighbour geometry as the grey graph layer.
Measured airtime and beacon-derived competition remain distinguishable; the
capacity proxy is not measured goodput. The default heavy-congestion boundary
is 70/100. Wide usable blocks are preferred; 40/20 MHz are considered when wide
space is crowded or cannot fit. Narrowing must improve estimated capacity or
spectrum separation. Allowed transmit power only breaks equal ties.
The policy is a bounded heuristic, not a guarantee of maximum client throughput.
At most four radios and 100000 search nodes are supported. Unsupported HE/EHT,
MLO and multi-interface arrangements are not managed as if they were validated.

DFS remains enforced by the kernel and hostapd. A compatible empty radio can
perform CAC while another serves clients. Shared clearance must be confirmed;
there is no fabricated clearance. The helper's original configuration is restored.
Radar or an unexpected driver channel change invalidates the old operation and
reconciles the actual legal placement, instead of treating the old target as an
outage. Hardware radar behavior still requires live validation on each platform.

radar.uc keeps at most 128 recent incidents, combining duplicate shared-PHY
notifications within five seconds. Each incident covers its affected spectrum,
regardless of primary channel, radio or candidate width. The default half-life
is six hours. Repeated independent events strengthen avoidance; old incidents
fade. No decay can shorten a kernel prohibition or replace mandatory CAC.
Subtle amber bands use the same decay in the Chart.js history plot, with no
lightning markers or extra outlines. The legend shows detection, one, two and
three half-lives using the current setting. This is remembered interruption risk,
not the probability of a future detection.

Handoff escalation is uniform: gentle, stronger, then optionally one bounded
disconnect. An appropriate live AP on the same network and band is required.
If handoff cannot finish, the common fallback clears temporary bans and lets
clients reconnect to the changing radio after its brief restart. A temporary
return ban defaults to 20 seconds; forced retries are limited to one per client
per five minutes. Protected clients are never forced. Client choice and brief
interruptions remain possible. The package never promises seamless roaming.
Radio Preference pauses on reserved radios, yields completely during channel
operations, and resumes afterwards. Its attempt budget expires; clients without
measurement support may receive a gentle advisory, never an unmeasured strong
request. Ordinary preference is not a throughput optimizer or forced steering.

Verified channel placements and radar memory are checkpointed at most once per
six hours, separately from the history archive below. Channel checkpoints occur
only while no scan/move is active and preserve administrator edits. Power loss
can lose recent observations or return to an older saved channel. No radio
movement causes an immediate wireless-config commit. Same-boot service restarts
retain queued placements and recent radar incidents in RAM, even if optional
graph history is disabled.

History uses a recent RAM buffer and immutable flash batches:
- Retention dropdown: 1, 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 168 or 200 hours.
  Default remains 24 hours. The Show dropdown follows the selected retention.
- /tmp/wifi-coordinator-history.json: up to 1 MiB of recent data in tmpfs.
- /etc/wifi-coordinator/batch-TIMESTAMP.json: one new file per six-hour offload.
  Flash archive size is determined by retention and recorded activity, with no
  fixed archive-size cap. Existing completed batches are never rewritten.
- Five-minute traffic summaries, each spectrum survey with original per-frequency
  measurements/times, actions and channel/width residence intervals retain their
  detail throughout retention. No two-hour aggregation or per-list count caps.
- Routine offload at most once per six hours when observations have changed. Copy only
  new/extended records, then keep the most recent hour cached in RAM. Boundary
  snapshots are merged by stable key, avoiding double-counted partial summaries.
- Delete only wholly expired batches during scheduled maintenance. Display uses
  the exact selected cutoff; physical deletion can lag because a batch straddles
  that cutoff and maintenance runs every six hours. Clear removes all immediately.
- A 4 MiB free-space reserve protects normal router operations. If a batch would
  breach that reserve or a write fails, leave previous batches intact, retain RAM
  and report the failure; retry after six hours. Never delete unexpired archives
  to force a batch to fit. A full RAM buffer reports any dropped records.
- Writes use a temporary file, sync, atomic rename and sync. Restarts, page reads
  and normal sampling never force an offload. The browser caches closed batches
  and reads new batches plus the RAM file on later refreshes.
- Existing history.json from 0.4.0 remains readable and byte-identical until it
  expires. Already aggregated or discarded old records cannot be reconstructed.
- Orderly shutdown makes one final checkpoint of pending history and radar memory.
  Repeating shutdown without new data makes no new archive. Archives survive reboot.
  Unexpected power loss can lose data since the last checkpoint (normally up to
  six hours, longer if writes failed).
- No client addresses, SSIDs, passwords, browsing activity or raw network scans
  are archived. Spectrum history is anonymous frequency-bin data.
- Turning recording off pauses new observations and preserves existing records.
  Retention still applies. Clear recorded history is the explicit delete action;
  it removes graph/action history but preserves operational radar-avoidance memory.
- No automatic export or remote telemetry. UCI defaults commit only real changes.
  Verified radio configuration changes still save as before.

Runtime steering state separately retains a bounded, expiring set of client
addresses for retry cooldowns. This is operational state, not exported history.
Traffic graphs show actual traffic carried, not available link capacity.
The channel-comparison panel keeps its expanded/collapsed state during polling.
Recent actions uses the same retention and Show window, with no separate count cap.

Install: trust radio-prefer-public.pem in /etc/apk/keys, then upload the APK in
LuCI System -> Software. The public key is already installed on the target.
Keep signature verification enabled. Official feed dependencies must be present
or downloadable. This merged package conflicts with the old standalone packages;
remove those first if migrating another installation, preserving its settings.
They have already been replaced on the target router.

Firmware upgrades: keep settings and reinstall the compatible APK/dependencies
as necessary. Preservation entries are included; a firmware upgrade has not been
tested. The package is local, not an official OpenWrt feed or Attended Sysupgrade
package. Private signing keys and router configuration backups are not releases.

Source modules live in ../channel-coordinator/files and ../radio-prefer/files.
Build with native apk v3: SIGN_KEY=/private/path APK_TOOL=/path/apk.static
sh build-apk.sh. Keep the signing key outside source and release directories.

Graphs use a privately scoped local Chart.js 4.5.1 library (MIT), bundled in
the APK. No CDN or browser internet access is needed. Traffic curves have no
fill. Radio ribbons use a light transparent fill and span their occupied width
on one frequency-proportional channel axis with the inter-band gap removed.
Grey spectrum surveys are regular Chart.js datasets behind the radio ribbons.
Each channel-level result extends until its next measurement, with the latest
shown to the end of the displayed timeline. There is no layer checkbox or
separate freshness cutoff. Hover identifies the actual measurement time.
The grey range runs from near-white to medium-dark grey. It estimates congestion
from overlapping Wi-Fi signals and advertised load, not exact airtime.

Partial surveys: available batches run even when another batch has no eligible
scanner. Every successful portion is saved immediately. Later deferral or scan
failure cannot discard earlier history. One sweep shares a compact record;
per-bin time offsets preserve the actual measurement times. Each survey record represents a sweep rather than a small batch. Automatic channel decisions
require full fresh coverage for the radio being moved; a partial sweep holds any radio whose eligible band is incomplete.
Manual --evaluate performs one paced survey and remains observation-only.

Radio visibility: radio0 is magenta with left-slanting hatches, radio1 orange with vertical hatches, and radio2 blue with right-slanting hatches. Hatch backgrounds are transparent. Overlapping ribbons combine their patterns. White edging surrounds the coloured outlines; there are no centre rails. Dashed outlines indicate radar preparation. Survey shading runs from near-white to medium-dark grey; values and channel geometry are unchanged. Hatch swatches match the plot; traffic lines remain unfilled.
